Comparing Step-by-Step

To compare decimals, line up the decimal points and compare digit by digit, left to right.

0.4   >   0.25

0.4 = 0.40. Compare tenths first: 4 tenths is more than 2 tenths, so 0.4 is greater — even though 25 "looks" like a bigger number!

Comparing on a Number Line

Numbers further to the right are greater.

0.3   <   0.7

Tricky One: Different Digit Counts

Which is greater: 0.6 or 0.59?

0.60   >   0.59

Add a zero to 0.6 to make 0.60 — now both have hundredths. 60 hundredths is more than 59 hundredths.

Ask Zoey:

  • "Does adding a zero change the value of 0.6? Why or why not?"

Using <, >, and =

  • < means "less than"
  • > means "greater than"
  • = means "equal to"
0.50   =   0.5

Ask Zoey:

  • "Which symbol goes between 0.35 and 0.53?"

Ordering Decimals

Order from least to greatest: 0.6, 0.15, 0.45

0.15   →   0.45   →   0.6

Rewrite with the same number of decimal places first: 0.60, 0.15, 0.45 — now they're easy to line up and compare!

Real-World: Race Times

Three runners' times (in seconds, lower is faster): 12.4, 12.35, 12.5

12.35  <  12.4  <  12.5

The runner with 12.35 seconds wins!
