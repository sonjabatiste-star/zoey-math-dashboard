What Is a Decimal?

A decimal is a way to show part of a whole, using a decimal point.

0.7

This says "seven tenths" — 7 out of 10 equal parts of a whole.

Ask Zoey:

  • "What do you think comes right after the decimal point?"

Tenths

When a whole is split into 10 equal parts, each part is one tenth (0.1).

3 out of 10 shaded = 0.3

Ask Zoey:

  • "How would we show 0.6 on this strip?"

Hundredths

When a whole is split into 100 equal parts, each part is one hundredth (0.01).

23 out of 100 shaded = 0.23

Ask Zoey:

  • "Which is bigger: one tenth or one hundredth?"

Decimals & Money

Money is a decimal system! Dollars are wholes, and cents are hundredths.

$3.45 = 3 dollars, 4 dimes, 5 pennies
3.45 → 3 wholes, 4 tenths, 5 hundredths

Ask Zoey:

  • "If you have $2.60, how many dimes is that after the whole dollars?"

Reading Decimals

We read the digits after the decimal point by their place value.

0.7 → "seven tenths"
0.45 → "forty-five hundredths"

Ask Zoey:

  • "How would you read 0.08?"

Place Value Chart

Decimals have their own place value spots, just like whole numbers do.

Ones . Tenths Hundredths
  3   .   4      5

In 3.45 — the 4 is in the tenths place, and the 5 is in the hundredths place.

Ask Zoey:

  • "In 6.29, what place is the 9 in?"

Zero as a Placeholder

Sometimes a decimal needs a zero to hold a place.

0.05 = five hundredths (not five tenths!)

The 0 right after the decimal point shows there are no tenths.

Ask Zoey:

  • "Why isn't 0.05 the same as 0.5?"
