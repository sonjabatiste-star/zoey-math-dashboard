Division Is the Opposite of Multiplication

If we know a multiplication fact, we know a division fact too.

4 × 12 = 48   →   48 ÷ 4 = 12

Last week we broke apart numbers to multiply. This week we break apart numbers to divide!

Ask Zoey:

  • "If 6 × 9 = 54, what is 54 ÷ 6?"

Friendly Pieces: Breaking Apart the Dividend

Break the big number into two pieces that are easy to divide.

36 = 30 + 6
36 ÷ 3 → 30 ÷ 3 = 10  and  6 ÷ 3 = 2  →  10 + 2 = 12

Ask Zoey:

  • "Why did we split 36 into 30 and 6, instead of 20 and 16?"

The Area Model for Division

Let's solve 78 ÷ 3. Split 78 into 60 + 18 — both pieces divide evenly by 3.

60 ÷ 3
= 20
18 ÷ 3
= 6
20 + 6 = 26

  • "How is this the same as the multiplication area model from last week, but backwards?"

Repeated Subtraction

Another way: keep taking away groups until nothing is left. Solve 52 ÷ 4.

52 − 40 = 12   (10 groups of 4)
12 − 12 = 0   (3 groups of 4)
10 + 3 = 13 groups

Taking away big chunks (like 10 groups at a time) makes this much faster!

Ask Zoey:

  • "Why is taking away 10 groups at once smarter than taking away 1 group at a time?"

Bigger Numbers: 4-Digit Division

The same strategy works for 4-digit numbers! Solve 1,248 ÷ 4.

1,248 = 1,200 + 48
1,200 ÷ 4
= 300
48 ÷ 4
= 12
300 + 12 = 312

Check With Multiplication

Division has a built-in answer checker: multiply back!

1,248 ÷ 4 = 312   →   check: 312 × 4 = 1,248 ✓

If multiplying your answer by the divisor gives you the starting number, you got it right.

When It Doesn't Divide Evenly

Remember remainders from Week 5? They come back! Solve 95 ÷ 4.

95 = 80 + 15
80 ÷ 4
= 20
15 ÷ 4
= 3 R3
20 + 3 = 23, with 3 left over → 23 R3
Check: (23 × 4) + 3 = 92 + 3 = 95 ✓

Ask Zoey:

  • "Why do we add the remainder back in when we check?"

Real-World: Cookie Boxes

Zoey's bakery has 156 cookies to pack evenly into 6 boxes. How many cookies in each box?

156 ÷ 6 = ?
156 = 120 + 36
120 ÷ 6
= 20
36 ÷ 6
= 6
20 + 6 = 26 cookies per box  (check: 26 × 6 = 156 ✓)

Remainders in Real Life (Week 5 Callback)

95 students need rides in vans that hold 4 students each. How many vans?

95 ÷ 4 = 23 R3

23 full vans leave 3 students behind — they still need a ride, so we need 24 vans.
